Research Experience

My research focuses on medical AI and computer vision, with hands-on work in deep learning systems for chest X-ray analysis and prior engineering-focused research in robotics and materials reuse.

June 2025 - Present

Research Intern | Feinstein Institutes for Medical Research, Northwell Health

Division of Health Artificial Intelligence — Mentors: Dr. Theo Zanos and Dr. Nabil Ettehadi

Project: Multi-model deep learning ensemble for 14-label thoracic pathology classification on NIH ChestX-ray14

  • Built a nine-model PyTorch ensemble on Databricks for 14-label thoracic pathology classification across 112K NIH ChestX-ray14 images.
  • Designed a hybrid CNN–Vision Transformer architecture with residual squeeze-and-excitation stem blocks, mixed-precision training, and a cosine learning-rate schedule with warmup.
  • Built a dual-scale U-Net with Performer attention and EfficientNet fusion; diagnosed a branch-dominance failure suppressing the decoder’s contribution to AUROC and proposed an auxiliary-loss correction.
  • Stacked the base models with scikit-learn and XGBoost meta-learners trained on exported embeddings; resolved solver convergence failures through pipeline-level feature scaling.
  • Integrating RAD-DINO, a chest X-ray–pretrained ViT, as a tenth base model and replacing per-class weighted BCE with Asymmetric Loss.

Robotics

My Journey

My journey in FIRST began in FLL and evolved into FTC through the Forest Hills Robotics League. I have stayed in the FIRST community for years, growing from student programmer to team captain.

My Team

From 2023 to 2026, I was captain and lead programmer of team #8721 Mighty Mechanics, a community-based FTC team that represents NYC in high-level competition and outreach.

We qualified for the FTC World Championship in Houston in 2024 and 2026, were NYC Finalists in 2025, and won the NYC Championship and the FTC Control Award in 2026. In 2025, I was recognized as a FIRST Dean's List Semi-Finalist (NYC).

My Skillset

As lead programmer, I led development of Mighty Motion, a custom Java motion-control library that replaced open-source alternatives, with our own localization, motion profiling, holonomic pure pursuit, PID pose control, and inverse kinematics.

  • Fused GoBILDA Pinpoint odometry with Limelight 3A AprilTag poses through parallel 1D Kalman filters into a reusable localizer, and isolated a Limelight firmware defect returning NaN rotations in near-head-on pose solutions.
  • Cut autonomous waypoint overshoot by regressing braking distance against velocity and decelerating pre-emptively.
  • Built AutoAim, deriving a quadratic distance-to-RPM regression and a turret angle solver that reduced manual alignment to one action.
